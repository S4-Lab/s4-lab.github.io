## Abstract

**Background:** Passive temperature sensing without any active electronics on the sensing node is desirable for many embedded and remote applications where batteries are impractical.

**Methods:** We developed a wireless Johnson noise thermometry system that measures the thermal noise power emitted by a resistor to passively sense temperature, with no active transmitter required at the sensor.

**Results:** The system successfully demonstrates passive, battery-free wireless temperature sensing, enabling thermometry in resource-constrained environments.

**Conclusions:** This work shows that Johnson noise can be harnessed as a communication and sensing mechanism, opening new possibilities for passive IoT sensing systems.
